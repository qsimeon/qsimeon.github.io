I'm an Applied Scientist at Amazon FAI (formerly Amazon AGI), where I work on the quality of the expert-sourced data and evaluations used to train and test frontier language models. Most days that means building agent-based pipelines and LLM judges that audit STEM expert data, calibrating those judges against human experts, and building RL environments for coding, STEM reasoning, and tool use.

I grew up in St. Lucia, came to MIT for a B.S. in Computation and Cognition, and stayed for graduate work in Ed Boyden's Synthetic Neurobiology Group, where I earned an M.S. in Computational Neuroscience. There I built neural-network models of the C. elegans nervous system, measured how they scale with data and parameters, and assembled an open dataset that combines neural recordings from 11 public datasets (about 900 worms) with the available connectomes. I also spent a lot of time rebuilding a light-sheet microscope.

In 2025 I moved toward AI engineering. At Numenta I designed a way to grow a pretrained language model from 70 million to 1.4 billion parameters without losing what it had already learned. At MIT's Office of Research Computing and Data I supported researchers on the university's clusters and built tooling for running LLM agents on them.

On the side I build with coding agents. With Justin Wei and Yile Fan I built Octopus, a system where a coding agent writes and tests the driver for whatever hardware is plugged in. At a Sundai hackathon my team built GrowLab, a harness that lets a research agent search training schedules. I write about these projects on the blog.

Outside of work I enjoy reading fiction, hiking, and traveling to understand different cultures.
